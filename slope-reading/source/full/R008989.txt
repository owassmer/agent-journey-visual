The shape of that distribution is what tells you how certain the model is: concentrated on one outcome means a confident answer, spread out means an uncertain one. The answer\u2019s `confidence` property collapses that shape into a single number from 0 to 1, so you can threshold on it without doing the math yourself.\nL25: (Noul answers don\u2019t carry one.)\nL26: ##\nL27: \nL28: \ue200cite\ue2023\u2020\u200b\ue201 L29: \nL30: Confidence is derived from the probabilities\nL31: \nL32: `confidence` is a statistic computed from the probability distribution the answer already gives you. TypeSafe computes it for you and returns it on every Choice and Score answer, so the common case needs no extra work on your side.\nL33: A solid default: We provide `confidence` as a convenient measure that fits most use-cases, but you are never locked into our definition. Depending on what you are evaluating, a different measure may serve you better, which is exactly why we give you the full `probabilities` in the response. The pros and cons of different computations is a specialized topic that we\u2019ll keep to a separate cookbook rather than this page, and will add the link here when we do!\nL34: For a \ue200cite\ue2024\u2020Choice\ue201 , the distribution is `probabilities` across your options.
