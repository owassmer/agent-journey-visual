Rules for every question\n8|\n9|**What a question is.** One well-defined uncertainty, with the context that bears on it. An actor's decision is the usual form. A threshold on an actor's outcome (\"does the amount entered exceed $X?\") and a joint event (\"does the offering raise at least $X net by the close date?\") are questions too, when the event is defined. An event is split into separate questions only where its parts differ in conditions, timing, amounts or consequences.\n10|\n11|**What a probability means.** The probability that the defined event occurs, given the stated situation and the evidence available by the cutoff. It is model judgment, never an observed frequency.\n12|\n13|**Answers.** Each answer establishes its event and nothing else. \"No\" means the event does not occur in the stated interval. The next action is its own event, or an explicit state rule; a \"no\" never supplies it.\n14|\n15|**Consequences.** Every booked consequence follows from the defined event, the coherent state on the path, and an explicit legal, accounting or scenario rule, labelled by basis: Law, Record, Data, Arithmetic, or Scenario (a declared assumption with its sensitivity).\n16|\n17|**Two dates.** The evidence cutoff is 14 May 2024 for every question. The decision date is the question's own day on the path. The state holds only events reached on the path before the decision date, on every trajectory the question covers. The absence of a document from the record is absence, never a fact that something did not happen.\n18|\n19|**Grouping.** One question covers trajectories that share legal status, available actions and ability to pay the amount in question. A difference in any of the three splits the group. Within a group the state gives one representative trajectory's joint facts (date, cash, amounts owed, all from the same trajectory) and the group's range for each; the answer applies to the group as its average. Figures from different trajectories are never combined into one situation.\n20|\n21|**Typed values.** Zero is a value. \"Not applicable\" and \"not available\" are states, stated in words. Cash is never shown negative; obligations the balance does not cover are shown as unpaid (§2.2).\n22|\n23|**Wording.** The question keeps every qualification that defines its event: \"if any\", notice requirements, burdens, the amount and timing of a proposed payment, the security offered, deadlines, and the month's operating need where it bears on the decision. It carries no implementation language: no draws, trajectories, lags, class names, booking rules, or instructions about amounts. The judgment is stated as entered (\"a money judgment of $X entered on <date>\"); an amount band states its band.\n24|\n25|**Evidence.** Each question receives the record that bears on whether its event occurs: the conditions the event depends on, the positions, options and incentives of every party whose action shapes it, and the facts that bound it, whoever wrote them. The scope follows the event, not an actor. A threshold on the jury's award draws on the trial record and on what the parties said about the likely award; an offering's completion draws on the company's condition, its trading, its past offerings and the terms. Balance between the parties is not a goal, and no party's own statement is a prerequisite. Each item carries its author, date and status: court ruling, statement of intended proof, party argument, company disclosure, or third-party record. Where the event is decided on a closed record (the jury on the trial evidence, a court on the papers before it), an item that was not part of that record is marked as such: it can inform the forecast without having been before the decider. An item establishes only what it states, and the question states what the record establishes; it does not list what the record lacks. A precedent is evidence, not a guarantee. Party advocacy stays, attributed. Duplicates, material that does not bear on the event, and historical warnings presented as live conditions are removed. A record item is labelled as what it is, and one that cannot exist by the cutoff is not listed. A reading of the record carries its source date and is routed only where it bears on the event at the decision date.\n26|\n27|**Record and situation.** Record names the historical evidence and the legal and contractual sources, retrieved from the snapshot; a record item no finding fills is left out. Situation gives the events, amounts, balances, terms and statuses established on the path; it comes from the path state and is always stated.\n28|\n29|**Legal meaning is not a Jev question.** Where research settles a legal question, it is Law. Where it does not, the interpretation is a declared legal scenario (§3), held the same way along the whole path, with the alternative as a sensitivity.\n30|\n31|**The no-lawsuit view.** The ordinary view asks the same questions on the same record. The dispute's resolution on the review date, at no cost to the company, is a stated event on its paths.\n32|\n33|**Depth.** A question is asked only where its answer can change cash, timing, an obligation, or a later decision inside the horizon (to 10 Nov 2024) on some trajectory.\n34|\n35|**Shared instruction** (both profiles, `dispute_forecast` and `financing_forecast`):\n36|\n37|> Forecast the outcome specified in the question at the stated decision time, conditional on the supplied situation. Treat the situation's events and amounts as given. Use the supplied evidence available by the evidence cutoff, preserving each source's author, date and status. Apply the supplied legal or contractual provisions where relevant. Estimate the probabilities of the defined outcomes.\n38|\n39|The state distinguishes five kinds of content: historical evidence, party assertions, court findings, events assumed on this path, and earlier readings of the record.\n40|\n41|---\n42|\n43|## 2.
