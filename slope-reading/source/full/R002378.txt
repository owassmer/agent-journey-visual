Is it legible and intuitive for a lender new to the case?\n\n**Legible: yes.** Typography, contrast, spacing, and plain-language titles are all good. Every card names the decision-maker, which is a genuinely useful touch. Numbers are large enough to read at a glance.\n\n**Intuitive: only partly.** A lender can scan it, but can easily draw the wrong conclusions. The main problems:\n\n1. **Conditional probabilities look like unconditional ones.** This is the biggest risk. \"Akoustis files for bankruptcy once the notes are due: Files 51\" is presumably the probability *given that the situation arises*. A lender skimming for bankruptcy risk will read it as \"51% chance of bankruptcy.\" Nothing on the card says how likely the situation is to occur at all. A 51% on a branch reached 5% of the time matters much less than a 33% on one reached in most futures.\n\n2. **\"Asked in N situations\" is unexplained.**\n   - It is unclear what a \"situation\" is: a path, a tree node, or a scenario.\n   - If a question is asked in 102 situations, the single number shown might be an average, a weighted mean, or the answer in the most common case. Its range across situations is also hidden.\n   - A lender may mistake N for a sample size or a confidence measure.\n   - Some cards lack the line entirely, which silently implies \"asked once\" but is never stated.\n\n3. **Sequence and dependency are implied, not shown.** The copy says \"in the order they come up,\" but order within a column only hints at dependency. For example, \"Qorvo accepts the cut award\" only matters if the judge picks \"Cuts it\" on the card above. Nothing links them, and nothing says which columns interact. The noteholder demand, for instance, presumably depends on Nasdaq delisting in column 4.\n\n4. **The lender's core question is scattered.** Bankruptcy appears in four cards across two columns:\n   - \"files once notes are due\"\n   - \"noteholders force a bankruptcy\"\n   - \"files when cash gets tight\"\n   - \"files when cash runs out\"\n\n   The difference between \"gets tight\" and \"runs out\" is not explained. The view also has no bottom line, such as overall probability of bankruptcy or expected recovery on the $44M notes. That summary may live in \"The paths,\" but a new lender landing here does not get it.\n\n5. **No case context.** The view never explains the $31.3M, the $44M, or who Qorvo and Akoustis are. That is fine if another section covers it, but this view is not self-contained.\n\n## 5. Clutter, clipping, and confusing encodings\n\n**Clipping and layout**\n- The bottom row is cut off: \"Akoustis appeals\" in column 1 and \"Akoustis's response to a seizure\" in column 2 (title, actor, and part of its bar are visible, but no numbers). Some of this is the screenshot viewport. Still, there is no \"more below\" cue, and the two longest columns hold the cut-off content.\n- Column heights are very uneven. The judge and collection columns run past the fold while Nasdaq ends about two-thirds down and cash ends at about a third. That leaves a large empty block at the lower right, which makes the page feel lopsided and hides content that could have fit above the fold.\n- There is unused space to the right of the 1168px content area.\n\n**Encoding issues**\n- **The gray segment reads as an empty track.** Because the last outcome is gray, the bars look like progress bars (\"23% complete\") rather than a two-outcome split.
