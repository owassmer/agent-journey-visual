5	## What the product does, and why the criteria
6	
7	The demo is a proof of concept for Russell at Slope, a B2B payments and financing company. Slope pays a business's supplier invoices, and the business repays in monthly installments. Slope underwrites on connected bank data.
8	
9	Thesis: domain-informed probabilistic judgments turn unusual qualitative evidence into a useful, measurable financial signal.
10	
11	At a decision date D:
12	- An agent investigates the borrower's live legal dispute(s) from the public record available by D.
13	- An LLM judgment model ("Jev") reads that evidence. It gives conditional probabilities for developments in court (ruling, appeal, bond, payment, settlement, enforcement). It also gives them for consequences beyond the courtroom: covenant or judgment defaults, litigation spend, customer or supplier relationships, and operational restrictions.
14	- Code turns those probabilities into dated cash paths. It then simulates the borrower's cash and the lender's dated collections over about 180 days on Slope-style financing of the borrower's supplier invoices.
15	
16	The financing is sized from the borrower under Slope's published practice (`slope_practices.md`), not fixed in advance. The price for a stressed borrower sits in the upper part of Slope's risk-based range, at about 15–22% or more a year. For example, 3.7% over three monthly installments is about 22% a year.
17	
18	ChromaDex, the previous lead, failed because it had $27.7M of cash and an undrawn $10M revolver at about 9.5%. It would never rationally draw Slope financing, so no path reached the loan.
19

31	## Criteria
32	
33	All eight are hard requirements. Score each 2 (pass), 1 (partial) or 0 (fail), for a total out of 16. Criterion 8 is a pass/fail gate.
34	
35	1. **Borrower fit, judged against Slope's actual practice** (see `slope_practices.md`). The borrower must be:
36	   - a US SEC filer, so quarterly financials exist;
37	   - a buyer of inventory, components, contract manufacturing, ingredients or marketing from suppliers on invoice terms.
38	
39	   Industry is not a test. Slope finances wholesalers, manufacturers, distributors, B2B retail and marketplace sellers.
40	
41	   Slope's own eligibility rules apply at D:
42	   - at least 1 year in business;
43	   - at least 3 years in business for a line above $100k;
44	   - at least $100k of annual revenue;
45	   - no bankruptcy in the previous 5 years.
46	
47	   Scale is judged by whether a Slope line fits the business, not by a revenue band. See the loan-sizing rule under criterion 8(c).
48	
49	   Poor fits: biotech with no sales, banks, REITs, and companies so large that a Slope line is immaterial to them.
50	2. **A live dispute at D, with cash consequences likely within about 180 days.** Examples are a pending damages or fee award, a judgment subject to appeal and bonding, enforcement, a settlement payable in installments, an injunction or recall hitting sales, or a key customer or supplier withholding payment or supply. The borrower may be the defendant, the plaintiff with a counterclaim against it, or otherwise exposed. Choose D so the dispute is genuinely unresolved and the next development is days to months away.
51	3. **The public record before D is enough for Jev to read the dispute.** Any court counts. Valid public sources include:
52	   - state e-filing portals and public case-search sites, such as NYSCEF, other state portals, county court search and state appellate dockets;
53	   - free RECAP and CourtListener documents, opinions and orders;
54	   - the company's own SEC filings: legal-proceedings sections, 8-Ks, and settlement or forbearance agreements filed as exhibits;
55	   - appellate opinions;
56	   - arbitration awards or outcomes the company disclosed.
57	
58	   Arbitration is excluded only when its key facts cannot be recovered from public sources. Score this criterion on whether the record is sufficient, not on which court the case is in. Paid documents are not used: everything must come from a public, free source. Say exactly what you opened.
59	4. **The signal can reach the lender.** The dispute exposure is material relative to liquidity: at least about 25–50% of unrestricted cash, or the borrower discloses tight liquidity.
60	5. **At least two channels beyond the courtroom, evidenced in filings before D.** These can be:
61	   - a credit facility with covenants, cross-default or judgment-default terms (ideally with the agreement filed as an exhibit);
62	   - material litigation spend;
63	   - a counterparty that is a customer, supplier, licensor or competitor;
64	   - an injunction, recall or operational restriction;
65	   - restricted cash, escrow or bonding;
66	   - dependence on financing the dispute could impair.
67	6. **Known outcome within about 12 months after D**, from later filings, the docket or opinions.
68	7. **Evidence can be acquired** from free public sources: EDGAR, including exhibits, and court records reachable without payment.
69	8. **A rational Slope draw at D, even in a view built on bank data alone**, with a citation for each part:
70	   - (a) Cheaper liquidity is absent or constrained. There is no undrawn bank revolver, or the revolver is blocked by its borrowing base, covenants or a default. Alternatives that already cost double digits count as constrained: merchant cash advances, factoring, high-rate term debt, and convertibles that depend on share sales.
71	   - (b) Cash is tight relative to supplier invoices and operating outflows: a few months of outflows, or going-concern or runway language.
72	   - (c) Recurring supplier invoices exist, and the business gets a real Slope line under Slope's own sizing rule:
73	     - **Line** = 15–33% of monthly revenue after debt service. Use the low end for a stressed borrower.
74	     - **Draw** = one real, recurring supplier invoice, up to the line.
75	     - **Terms** = Slope's menu: net 30–90 days, or 2–3 monthly installments. Pick the option a business with this cash cycle would choose.
76	
77	     Slope approves up to $250k automatically; larger lines need manual review. The demo's former $2M is not a target: size the loan from the borrower.
78	   - (d) The dispute's cash consequences within about 180 days are material to that tight position.
79	
80	   A borrower already collapsing is a weaker demo than one that is stressed but plausibly fundable, because the bank data alone would decline it. Collapsing means cash below one month of outflows, or defaults already declared.
81	
82	**Preferences:** 2021–2025; a counterparty that is also a trade partner; several channels that interact; a less-famous case, since famous cases raise the risk that the model remembers the outcome.
83	
84	**Exclusions:** already in bankruptcy at D; securities or derivative suits only; disputes resolved before D; key facts recoverable only from sealed filings.

115	# Slope's lending sizing and practices, from public sources
116	
117	Compiled 25 Sep 2026 from Slope's own help center, product pages and press releases, plus two news reports. These describe current practice (help-center articles edited 2024–2025; the Amazon program dates from December 2025). Applying them to a historical decision date is a reconstruction: it asks what Slope, as it describes itself, would offer this business on D.
118	
119	## Who and what Slope finances
120	
121	- **Who.** B2B wholesalers, manufacturers and distributors; B2B retail; B2B and B2C marketplace sellers (Amazon, Walmart, Alibaba); invoicing and bill-payment platforms; B2B service providers. Industry is not an eligibility test. [P3, P1]
122	- **What.** Bill pay (Pay Later) finances "on an unsecured basis) revenue-generating invoices, such as inventory purchases and marketing spend". Slope pays the vendor within one business day. [H1]
123	- **Lender of record.** Loans are made by Lead Bank. The Amazon program is funded through a J.P. Morgan credit facility. Slope reports more than $500M of SMB originations on its own capital. [H1, P4, P6]
124	
125	## Eligibility [H2, H3]
126	
127	- At least 1 year in business and of banking history.
128	- At least 3 years in business for lines above $100k.
129	- Annual revenue of $100k or more.
130	- A personal guarantee is required below $5M of annual revenue.
131	- An owner FICO of about 625.
132	- **No bankruptcies in the last 5 years.**
133	- All business bank accounts connected through Plaid. Bank data is "core to our credit decision" and drives the limit, the pricing and eligibility.
134	- Factors considered: bank-account activity, revenue, leverage, business credit history and the owner's personal credit.
135	
136	## Line size [H1, H3, H5, P4, N1]
137	
138	- **Rule:** "Financing limits typically range from ~15% to approximately a third of your monthly revenue after debt service, depending on your credit profile."
139	- **Two limits:** a pre-approved line limit, and an order limit, which is the largest single financed transaction.
140	- **Approval:** automated up to $250k in the bill-pay flow (up to $100k in the DocuSketch flow). Larger lines need manual review and more information.
141	- **Upper end:** up to $5M in the Amazon program, "for the strongest profiles".
142	- **Reassessment:** lines are "reassess[ed] ... from time to time" and "can automatically upsize based on cash flow trends". SlopeScore describes dynamic line sizing that adjusts with real-time cash-flow data to prevent over-extension.
143	
144	## Terms [H1, H4, F1, P4]
145	
146	- **Bill pay:** net terms up to 90 days, or monthly installments up to 120 days. Net 90 and 120-day installments are for selected customers only.
147	- **Common installment option:** 2 equal payments over 60 days (day 30 and 60), or 3 equal payments over 90 days (day 30, 60 and 90).
148	- **Checkout FAQ:** 30 and 60-day net terms, or 60 and 90-day installments.
149	- **Amazon line of credit:** revolving; the borrower picks a 2–12 month term for each draw.
150	
151	## Price [H1, M1, N1, P4, F2]
152	
153	- Risk-based.
154	- As low as 0.99% for Net 30 and 2.99% for Net 90 for the best customers ("below 12% APR"). The bill-pay page says "as low as 1.60% on Net 30".
155	- Bloomberg reports Slope "often charges about 2.5% on a 60-day loan, ... about 15%" annualized.
156	- The Amazon line starts at 8.99% APR.
157	- No fee to apply or to keep a line open.
158	- Fees are prorated on early full repayment.
159	- Card repayment carries a 3.05% processing fee.
160	- "We do impose interest if you default on the loan."
161	
162	## Repayment [H6, H7]
163	
164	- The chosen payment method is debited automatically on each due date. Reminders start at least a week before.
165	- ACH repayments take about 3 business days to clear and free up the limit.
166	- As the line is repaid, capacity frees up for the next draw.
167	
168	## What this means for case selection and the demo
169	
170	- **The loan is sized from the borrower.**
171	  - Line = 15–33% of monthly revenue after debt service.
172	  - A stressed borrower sits at the low end of that range.
173	  - A single draw is one real supplier invoice, capped by the order limit.
174	  - Rough scale:
175	    - $100k–$250k lines (automated approval) fit roughly $4M–$20M of annual revenue.
176	    - $0.5M–$2M lines fit roughly $20M–$160M, before debt service.
177	  - The demo's $2M is at the manual-review top of the bill-pay range.
178	- **Revenue floor.** $100k of annual revenue is Slope's floor. The $10M floor in our criteria was ours, not Slope's.
179	- **Industry.** A business that buys inventory, components or marketing on invoices qualifies, whatever it makes.
180	- **Price at our kind of borrower.** For the stressed borrower the demo needs, the realistic price is the upper part of the range. The supplied 3.7% over 3 monthly installments is about 22% APR on the average balance, which is consistent.
181	- **Bankruptcy.** A bankruptcy in the 5 years before D makes the borrower ineligible.
182	- **The lender relationship is a reusable line, not one loan.** Draws are repaid over 30–120 days, and the line is reassessed on bank data. A lender-side channel follows from this: when cash flow deteriorates, the line is resized and exposure falls on later draws. That is how Slope itself would respond to the event.
