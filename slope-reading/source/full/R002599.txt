These describe current practice (help-center articles edited 2024–2025; the Amazon program dates from December 2025). Applying them to a historical decision date is a reconstruction: it asks what Slope, as it describes itself, would offer this business on D.\n4|\n5|## Who and what Slope finances\n6|\n7|- **Who.** B2B wholesalers, manufacturers and distributors; B2B retail; B2B and B2C marketplace sellers (Amazon, Walmart, Alibaba); invoicing and bill-payment platforms; B2B service providers. Industry is not an eligibility test. [P3, P1]\n8|- **What.** Bill pay (Pay Later) finances \"on an unsecured basis) revenue-generating invoices, such as inventory purchases and marketing spend\". Slope pays the vendor within one business day. [H1]\n9|- **Lender of record.** Loans are made by Lead Bank. The Amazon program is funded through a J.P. Morgan credit facility. Slope reports more than $500M of SMB originations on its own capital. [H1, P4, P6]\n10|\n11|## Eligibility [H2, H3]\n12|\n13|- At least 1 year in business and of banking history.\n14|- At least 3 years in business for lines above $100k.\n15|- Annual revenue of $100k or more.\n16|- A personal guarantee is required below $5M of annual revenue.\n17|- An owner FICO of about 625.\n18|- **No bankruptcies in the last 5 years.**\n19|- All business bank accounts connected through Plaid. Bank data is \"core to our credit decision\" and drives the limit, the pricing and eligibility.\n20|- Factors considered: bank-account activity, revenue, leverage, business credit history and the owner's personal credit.\n21|\n22|## Line size [H1, H3, H5, P4, N1]\n23|\n24|- **Rule:** \"Financing limits typically range from ~15% to approximately a third of your monthly revenue after debt service, depending on your credit profile.\"\n25|- **Two limits:** a pre-approved line limit, and an order limit, which is the largest single financed transaction.\n26|- **Approval:** automated up to $250k in the bill-pay flow (up to $100k in the DocuSketch flow). Larger lines need manual review and more information.\n27|- **Upper end:** up to $5M in the Amazon program, \"for the strongest profiles\".\n28|- **Reassessment:** lines are \"reassess[ed] ... from time to time\" and \"can automatically upsize based on cash flow trends\". SlopeScore describes dynamic line sizing that adjusts with real-time cash-flow data to prevent over-extension.\n29|\n30|## Terms [H1, H4, F1, P4]\n31|\n32|- **Bill pay:** net terms up to 90 days, or monthly installments up to 120 days. Net 90 and 120-day installments are for selected customers only.\n33|- **Common installment option:** 2 equal payments over 60 days (day 30 and 60), or 3 equal payments over 90 days (day 30, 60 and 90).\n34|- **Checkout FAQ:** 30 and 60-day net terms, or 60 and 90-day installments.\n35|- **Amazon line of credit:** revolving; the borrower picks a 2–12 month term for each draw.\n36|\n37|## Price [H1, M1, N1, P4, F2]\n38|\n39|- Risk-based.\n40|- As low as 0.99% for Net 30 and 2.99% for Net 90 for the best customers (\"below 12% APR\"). The bill-pay page says \"as low as 1.60% on Net 30\".\n41|- Bloomberg reports Slope \"often charges about 2.5% on a 60-day loan, ... about 15%\" annualized.\n42|- The Amazon line starts at 8.99% APR.\n43|- No fee to apply or to keep a line open.\n44|- Fees are prorated on early full repayment.\n45|- Card repayment carries a 3.05% processing fee.\n46|- \"We do impose interest if you default on the loan.\"\n47|\n48|## Repayment [H6, H7]\n49|\n50|- The chosen payment method is debited automatically on each due date. Reminders start at least a week before.\n51|- ACH repayments take about 3 business days to clear and free up the limit.\n52|- As the line is repaid, capacity frees up for the next draw.\n53|\n54|## What this means for case selection and the demo\n55|\n56|- **The loan is sized from the borrower.**\n57|  - Line = 15–33% of monthly revenue after debt service.\n58|  - A stressed borrower sits at the low end of that range.\n59|  - A single draw is one real supplier invoice, capped by the order limit.\n60|  - Rough scale:\n61|    - $100k–$250k lines (automated approval) fit roughly $4M–$20M of annual revenue.\n62|    - $0.5M–$2M lines fit roughly $20M–$160M, before debt service.\n63|  - The demo's $2M is at the manual-review top of the bill-pay range.\n64|- **Revenue floor.** $100k of annual revenue is Slope's floor. The $10M floor in our criteria was ours, not Slope's.\n65|- **Industry.** A business that buys inventory, components or marketing on invoices qualifies, whatever it makes.\n66|- **Price at our kind of borrower.** For the stressed borrower the demo needs, the realistic price is the upper part of the range. The supplied 3.7% over 3 monthly installments is about 22% APR on the average balance, which is consistent.\n67|- **Bankruptcy.** A bankruptcy in the 5 years before D makes the borrower ineligible.\n68|- **The lender relationship is a reusable line, not one loan.** Draws are repaid over 30–120 days, and the line is reassessed on bank data. A lender-side channel follows from this: when cash flow deteriorates, the line is resized and exposure falls on later draws.
