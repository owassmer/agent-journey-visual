**BLOCKING**
1. **The notes' judgment-default rule (L11) rests on authorities that don't decide it.** *Banister v. Davis* and *Osterneck* say post-trial motions suspend finality "for purposes of an appeal"; L11 quotes "suspends the finality" and drops that phrase.
   - Execution was available from 20 Jun under Rule 62(a), and the model's own pre-ruling execution branch depends on that.
   - §7.01(i) measures the default by whether execution is stayed, which only works for a judgment that can be executed.
   - The only research on the point (R2j) found no New York decision and recorded it as OPEN, leaning the other way.
   - Effect: the 19 Aug ripe date disappears on every path, and the default now ripens around 7 Dec at the median ruling. Fix: put L11 back to OPEN, decided by the holders' question (H1), with 19 Aug restored.
2. **If L11 stands, the 60 days start from the wrong ruling.** The code uses the last money-motion ruling, which leaves out the injunction motion (D.I. 608) that the model itself treats as tolling. About one trajectory in four ripens early. DECOMPOSITION §2 also misstates FRAP 4(a)(4)(A) as "the last order changing the judgment".
3. **The damages question (J2) gets the JMOL standard and no new-trial standard.** Under *Roebuck* the new-trial standard is easier to meet than JMOL, and *Gumbs* and *Williamson* set the excessive-damages and weight-of-evidence tests. Giving Jev the strictest standard pushes the answer toward "verdict stands". Fix: add a Rule 59(a) rule with those cases and use it for J2.
4. **The noteholders' involuntary petition (H3) ignores the indenture's no-action clause.** §7.06 bars holders from "any proceeding … for the appointment of a receiver or trustee" unless 25% ask the trustee, offer indemnity and the trustee fails to act within 60 days. §7.07 protects only payment on the notes' stated due dates. Courts are split (*Marcade* barred a petition; *Envirodyne* allowed one). The model has holders file on the acceleration day, when most such petitions would in fact fall after 17 Dec.
5. **After the ruling, the company's response to enforcement (A4) doesn't follow the design.**
   - It is asked on the ruling date, 30 or more days before the levy it responds to.
   - After "seek a sale or financing" it is never asked again, although DECOMPOSITION T1-d requires a re-ask at the levy and the ripe date.
   - So after the ruling, "seek" and "neither" become the same branch. This shifts cases from "frozen by a filing" to "levied, then unpaid"; I did not measure by how much.
6. **Some basis strings in `dispute_model.json` misstate the law.** For example, it says "a pending stay motion may suspend enforcement", but under Rule 62(b) a stay takes effect only on approval. Nothing reads these strings, so the fix is text only.

**NOTES**
