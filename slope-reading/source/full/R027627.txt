## What the product does (why the criteria)
Thesis: domain-informed probabilistic judgments turn unusual qualitative evidence into a useful, measurable financial signal. At a decision date D, an agent investigates the borrower's live legal dispute(s) from public filings and court records available by D; an LLM judgment model reads the evidence and gives conditional probabilities for future developments (ruling, appeal, bond, payment, settlement, enforcement), and also for consequences BEYOND the courtroom (credit-facility covenants/defaults, litigation spend, customer or supplier relationships, operational restrictions). Code turns those into dated cash paths and simulates the borrower's cash and the lender's dated collections on Slope-style financing of the borrower's supplier invoices over ~180 days. The current case (ChromaDex, 19 Aug 2024; fee award ~$9.8M vs $27.7M cash) failed one key test: the borrower is so liquid that no path reaches the loan, and its non-courtroom channels are thin within 180 days. We need a case where the signal can plausibly reach a realistic loan and where beyond-courtroom channels are evidenced.

## Criteria
Hard requirements (all):
1. **Borrower fit:** US public company (SEC filer, so quarterly financials exist) that buys inventory/finished goods/services from suppliers on invoice terms, the kind of business Slope finances: consumer products, food & beverage, supplements, DTC/e-commerce brands, apparel, distribution, light manufacturing. Roughly $10M–$500M annual revenue. Not a bank, biotech with no sales, REIT or mega-cap.
2. **A live dispute at D with cash consequences likely inside ~180 days after D:** e.g. a pending damages or fee award amount, a judgment subject to appeal and bonding, enforcement, a settlement payable in installments, an injunction or recall affecting sales, a key customer/supplier dispute withholding payments or supply. D should be chosen so the dispute is genuinely unresolved on D (the next development is days to months away) and the evidence up to D is public.
3. **Court record available before D:** federal docket with free RECAP documents on CourtListener (orders, judgments, key motions) or an accessible state docket. Check actual availability (CourtListener API: https://www.courtlistener.com/api/rest/v4/search/?q=...&type=r works anonymously via curl, ~50 req/hour; documents at storage.courtlistener.com).
4. **The signal can reach the lender:** the dispute exposure (amount at stake, bond, lost revenue) is material relative to the borrower's liquidity, e.g. at least ~25-50% of unrestricted cash, or the borrower discloses tight liquidity (going-concern doubt, covenant pressure, dependence on a credit line or equity raise). A plausible Slope exposure (recurring supplier invoices of ~$100k-$5M) could be affected.
5. **Beyond-courtroom channels evidenced in pre-D filings** (at least two): a credit facility with financial covenants, cross-default or judgment-default terms (ideally with the agreement filed as an exhibit); material litigation spend; the counterparty is a customer, supplier, licensor or competitor whose relationship affects receipts or supply; an injunction/recall/operational restriction; restricted cash, escrow or bonding requirements; dependence on financing that the dispute could impair.
6. **Known outcome within ~12 months after D** in later public filings or the docket (for a later reveal).
7. **Evidence acquirable:** SEC filings on EDGAR (including exhibits) and court documents from RECAP.
Preferences: recent (2021-2025); decision date inside a live phase; a counterparty that is also a trade partner; several channels that interact (e.g. a judgment that also trips a covenant and strains supplier terms); a less-famous case (widely reported mega-cases raise the risk the model remembers the outcome).
Exclusions: already in bankruptcy at D; securities class actions/derivative suits with no near-term cash consequence; disputes resolved before D; cases whose key facts only appear in sealed filings.
