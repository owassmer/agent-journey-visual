Round 1 is recorded in `../shortlist.md` and `../notes_*.md`. Read `../shortlist.md` before you start. Its scoring and dossier format is the format for this round.\n\n## What the product does, and why the criteria\n\nThe demo is a proof of concept for Russell at Slope, a B2B payments and financing company. Slope pays a business's supplier invoices, and the business repays in monthly installments. Slope underwrites on connected bank data.\n\nThesis: domain-informed probabilistic judgments turn unusual qualitative evidence into a useful, measurable financial signal.\n\nAt a decision date D:\n- An agent investigates the borrower's live legal dispute(s) from the public record available by D.\n- An LLM judgment model (\"Jev\") reads that evidence. It gives conditional probabilities for developments in court (ruling, appeal, bond, payment, settlement, enforcement). It also gives them for consequences beyond the courtroom: covenant or judgment defaults, litigation spend, customer or supplier relationships, and operational restrictions.\n- Code turns those probabilities into dated cash paths. It then simulates the borrower's cash and the lender's dated collections over about 180 days on Slope-style financing of the borrower's supplier invoices. The supplied terms are 3.7% over three monthly installments, which is roughly 20% or more a year.\n\nChromaDex, the previous lead, failed because it had $27.7M of cash and an undrawn $10M revolver at about 9.5%. It would never rationally draw Slope financing, so no path reached the loan.\n\n## Why round 2 exists\n\nRound 1 ruled out state-court cases for reasons that came from its tools, not from the product:\n- Shell access was blocked.\n- A shared web-search budget ran out.\n- In practice, \"accessible docket\" came to mean \"reachable through CourtListener\".\n\nSmall companies' disputes with their suppliers, co-manufacturers and customers usually land in state court or arbitration. Round 1's strongest consumer-shaped candidates were all in state court: MusclePharm, Massimo and Tel-Instrument. Barfresh had been deferred earlier for \"no accessible docket\" when its case was simply in California state court.\n\nRound 2 treats the state-court record and the company's own disclosures as first-class evidence.\n\n## Criteria\n\nAll eight are hard requirements. Score each 2 (pass), 1 (partial) or 0 (fail), for a total out of 16. Criterion 8 is a pass/fail gate.\n\n1. **Borrower fit.** A US SEC filer, so quarterly financials exist. It buys inventory, finished goods, ingredients, components or contract manufacturing from suppliers on invoice terms. Good fits are consumer products, food and beverage, supplements, personal care, DTC or e-commerce brands, apparel, home goods, distribution and light manufacturing. Revenue is ideally $10M\u2013$200M; up to $500M is allowed but disfavoured. Poor fits are deep-tech fabs, biotech with no sales, banks, REITs and mega-caps.\n2. **A live dispute at D, with cash consequences likely within about 180 days.** Examples are a pending damages or fee award, a judgment subject to appeal and bonding, enforcement, a settlement payable in installments, an injunction or recall hitting sales, or a key customer or supplier withholding payment or supply. The borrower may be the defendant, the plaintiff with a counterclaim against it, or otherwise exposed. Choose D so the dispute is genuinely unresolved and the next development is days to months away.\n3. **The public record before D is enough for Jev to read the dispute.** Any court counts. Valid public sources include:\n   - state e-filing portals and public case-search sites, such as NYSCEF, other state portals, county court search and state appellate dockets;\n   - free RECAP and CourtListener documents, opinions and orders;\n   - the company's own SEC filings: legal-proceedings sections, 8-Ks, and settlement or forbearance agreements filed as exhibits;\n   - appellate opinions;\n   - arbitration awards or outcomes the company disclosed.\n\n   Arbitration is excluded only when its key facts cannot be recovered from public sources. Score this criterion on whether the record is sufficient, not on which court the case is in. Paid documents are not used: everything must come from a public, free source. Say exactly what you opened.\n4. **The signal can reach the lender.** The dispute exposure is material relative to liquidity: at least about 25\u201350% of unrestricted cash, or the borrower discloses tight liquidity.\n5. **At least two channels beyond the courtroom, evidenced in filings before D.** These can be:\n   - a credit facility with covenants, cross-default or judgment-default terms (ideally with the agreement filed as an exhibit);\n   - material litigation spend;\n   - a counterparty that is a customer, supplier, licensor or competitor;\n   - an injunction, recall or operational restriction;\n   - restricted cash, escrow or bonding;\n   - dependence on financing the dispute could impair.\n6. **Known outcome within about 12 months after D**, from later filings, the docket or opinions.\n7. **Evidence can be acquired** from free public sources: EDGAR, including exhibits, and court records reachable without payment.\n8. **A rational Slope draw at D, even in a view built on bank data alone**, with a citation for each part:\n   - (a) Cheaper liquidity is absent or constrained. There is no undrawn bank revolver, or the revolver is blocked by its borrowing base, covenants or a default. Alternatives that already cost double digits count as constrained: merchant cash advances, factoring, high-rate term debt, and convertibles that depend on share sales.\n   - (b) Cash is tight relative to supplier invoices and operating outflows: a few months of outflows, or going-concern or runway language.\n   - (c) Recurring supplier invoices exist at a scale Slope would finance, about $100k\u2013$5M per invoice or per month.\n   - (d) The dispute's cash consequences within about 180 days are material to that tight position.\n\n   A borrower already collapsing is a weaker demo than one that is stressed but plausibly fundable, because the bank data alone would decline it. Collapsing means cash below one month of outflows, or defaults already declared.\n\n**Preferences:** 2021\u20132025; a counterparty that is also a trade partner; several channels that interact; a less-famous case, since famous cases raise the risk that the model remembers the outcome.\n\n**Exclusions:** already in bankruptcy at D; securities or derivative suits only; disputes resolved before D; key facts recoverable only from sealed filings.\n\n## Method notes\n\n- **SEC requests:** use User-Agent `Owen Wassmer owassmer1@gmail.com` and keep to at most about 5 requests a second.\n- **EDGAR full-text search:** `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index?q=%22PHRASE%22&forms=10-Q&dateRange=custom&startdt=2021-01-01&enddt=2025-09-30`.
