Headline findings\n\n1. **The ATM is the only pre-arranged channel, and it is best-efforts with no obligation to sell.** The re-activated ATM has $48.0M of registered capacity. Sales can settle two business days after each trade. The agents have no obligation to sell and can suspend at any time (§1.1).\n2. **The baby-shelf cap (I.B.6) most likely did *not* bind on 14 May 2024.** S-3 eligibility is re-tested at each Section 10(a)(3) update, which in practice is the 10-K filing (CDI 114.02, 114.04). At the FY2023 10-K filing on 6 Sep 2023, Akoustis's float was about $199M at the 60-day high close, and at least about $105M even at the lowest close in that window. That is well above $75M, so it was an I.B.1 filer until its next 10-K (FY2024, due around the end of September 2024). Two pieces of evidence fit this reading. The ATM Sales Agreement represents I.B.1 status at every sale. The January 2024 prospectus carries no I.B.6 cover calculation, which Instruction 7 would require if the offering had been made under I.B.6 (§2.3).\n3. **The model's $9.7M is wrong on each input even if the cap applied.** It uses total shares instead of non-affiliate shares, the 1 May close ($0.60) instead of the 60-day high close ($0.6135 on 21 Mar), and $10.0M instead of the $11.5M gross January sale. The binding constraint before September 2024 was not I.B.6 (§2.4–2.5).\n4. **The real near-term limits were market depth, contract conditions and law, not the one-third rule.** The limits are below; items (c) and (d) are detailed in §5.\n   - (a) Market depth: dollar ADV was about $0.34M a day (15 Mar–14 May 2024).
