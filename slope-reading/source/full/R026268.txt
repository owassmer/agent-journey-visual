# TypeSafe's Stated Use Cases and Jev's Capabilities

## Intended Use Cases

TypeSafe identifies several applications for Jev:

- **"AI-Powered Workflows / smart if-statements"** - structured outputs functioning as fuzzy decision rules for classification, routing, scoring, extraction, or branching
- **Map-reducing over big data** to generate features and insights
- **Real-time applications** where 100ms latency enables AI integration without compromising user experience
- **Verification tasks** including scoring, judging, guardrailing, and detecting jailbreaks

## Probabilities and Calibration

Jev outputs accompany answers with calibrated probabilities and confidence scores. The company emphasizes that "higher confidence means higher accuracy" and that responses are "more consistent: returns similar answers for similar inputs."

Regarding calibration measurement, TypeSafe states their workflow evaluations use "the average of the smartest models...as reference probabilities" rather than ground truth labels—an approach they designed to reflect production workload authenticity.

## Limitations

The documentation notes Jev "**gives up string generation**, it's optimized for structured outputs." The model supports cardinality up to 255 for choices, requiring two-stage scoring for higher-cardinality decisions.

**Note:** The document doesn't explicitly mention "Choice" and "Noul" primitives or detailed recommended patterns beyond workflow decomposition.
