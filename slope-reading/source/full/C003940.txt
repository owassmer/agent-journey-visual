{"path": "/Users/owenwassmer/Desktop/Connor/Slope_Sparse_Events_Briefing.md", "content": "# A lawsuit, a credit line, and 512 futures\n\n*A briefing on the Slope Sparse Events build: the case, the domain, and every component, in the order the money moves. Written 29 Sep 2026, while the final run is in progress.*\n\n---\n\n## Summary\n\n- **The problem.** Russell's problem is a lender's problem. Bank data tells you how a business has been doing. It tells you nothing about the lawsuit that decides how the business will be doing. Lawsuits are rare across a loan book, so they make terrible features for a population model. For the one borrower who has one, though, the lawsuit can be the whole credit.\n- **The build.** It takes one real borrower (Akoustis Technologies) on one real day (14 May 2024, mid-trial against Qorvo), with a Slope-style line already drawn. It produces a forecast of that line's dated cash flows from everything public that day:\n  - what gets collected, when, and what is stuck if the company files;\n  - how much of that is the lawsuit, and how much is just an expensive chip company burning cash.\n- **How.** An agent reads the public record and files evidence into named slots. Jev, a narrow judgment model, answers one well-defined question at a time with a probability. Code owns every date, dollar and rule, turns each answer into dated cash, and runs that cash through Slope's debit every day across 512 plausible operating futures.\n- **What it does not do.** It does not tell Slope whether to lend. It tells Slope what the lawsuit does to the money, and which judgment matters most.\n- **Watchpoints.**\n  - Jev's probabilities are model judgment, not frequencies.\n  - It is one case.\n  - The bank data is synthetic, reconstructed from the company's filings and labelled once.\n  - Everything else is sourced.\n\n---\n\n## Part 1. The case\n\n### The borrower\n\nAkoustis makes RF filters, the small components in a phone or a Wi-Fi router that let the right frequencies through and keep the wrong ones out. Its technology is bulk acoustic wave (BAW) filters on single-crystal material, made in its own fab in upstate New York. Its headquarters are in North Carolina.\n\nIt is the classic small-cap deep-tech story. There is real technology and real customers, and revenue is much smaller than the cost of running a fab. In our reconstructed bank feed, receipts run about $2.64M a month against about $5.23M of outflows, a burn of roughly $2.6M a month.\n\nOn 13 May 2024 it filed its 10-Q. That filing:\n- carried going-concern language: substantial doubt about the company's ability to continue as a going concern;\n- said it was re-activating its at-the-market equity program;\n- said an adverse outcome in the Qorvo trial \"will severely constrain\" its liquidity.\n\nThe earnings call was the same day, and the stock fell from about $0.57 to $0.42. On 14 May it closed at $0.44. We reconstruct cash that day at about $11.3M, against a 30-day operating need of about $2.8M.\n\n### The lawsuit\n\n*Qorvo v. Akoustis*, D. Del. No. 1:21-cv-01417, was filed in October 2021. Qorvo is the incumbent in BAW filters, and Akoustis hired a lot of its people. Qorvo's claims:\n- trade-secret misappropriation (federal DTSA and North Carolina law);\n- civil conspiracy;\n- false advertising under the Lanham Act;\n- unfair and deceptive trade practices (UDTPA);\n- patent infringement.\n\nThe jury trial started on 6 May 2024. So on 14 May, our review date, the trial is on day 7 and nobody knows the answer.\n\nWhat was knowable on 14 May:\n- **What Qorvo is asking for.** The pretrial order (D.I. 543-1, 22 Apr) itemizes it:\n  - at least $66.1M of trade-secret unjust enrichment (\"head start\" damages: what Akoustis gained by getting there faster);\n  - $279,808 on the patents;\n  - $1,146,604 on false advertising;\n  - some other smaller items.\n- **What the jury will be asked.** The final verdict form (D.I. 580) asks, claim by claim, whether each is proven and, if so, how much:\n  - 1(a) trade secrets, 1(b) the amount, 1(c) willful and malicious, 1(d) exemplary damages (capped at twice 1(b) under the DTSA);\n  - 2 conspiracy, with 2(c) its actual-loss amount;\n  - 3 false advertising;\n  - 4 UDTPA, whose damages the court's order D.I. 590 had already barred;\n  - 5 patents.\n- **What the market thinks.** When the $66.1M claim became public on 22 Apr, the stock went *up*, from $0.49 to $0.60. The market was not pricing a large award. File that away; it matters in Part 4.\n\n### The debt that makes the lawsuit dangerous\n\nAkoustis has $44M of 6% convertible senior notes under a June 2022 indenture. The notes are where a lawsuit turns into a solvency problem, through three clauses.\n\n- **\u00a77.01(i), the judgment default.** It is an Event of Default if \"a final judgment or final judgments for the payment of money\" are entered against the company, remain \"undischarged, unpaid or unstayed\" for 60 days during which execution is not effectively stayed, and exceed $10.0M in aggregate, after notice from the trustee or holders of 25% of the notes. So a $9M judgment is a bad day; an $11M judgment starts a clock on $44M of debt. That $10M line appears again and again below.\n- **\u00a77.01(j)(v), general nonpayment.** It is an Event of Default if the company \"generally is not paying its debts as they become due.\" This is one of the bankruptcy-type defaults. Under \u00a77.02 those accelerate the notes automatically, with no declaration needed.\n- **\u00a77.06, the no-action clause.** Holders can't sue or petition on their own unless they have asked the trustee and the trustee has sat on it for 60 days. The bar doesn't apply to the (j) and (k) bankruptcy-type defaults.\n\nThe notes also pay interest twice a year: $1.32M, due 15 June. By default, \u00a716.02(c) pays it in \"Freely Tradeable\" shares, valued at 95% of the 10-day average price. New shares are only freely tradeable if registered, and the company registered 5,000,000 of them for interest on 13 May.\n\n### The listing\n\nNasdaq requires a $1.00 minimum bid price. Akoustis got its deficiency notice in October 2023, and its second compliance period ends 21 Oct 2024. By then it either gets back above $1 (in practice, a reverse split, which needs a shareholder vote it has not yet scheduled), or it gets delisted. It can request a hearing, which stays the suspension while a panel decides.\n\nDelisting matters to the notes. It is a Fundamental Change, which gives holders the right to make the company repurchase their notes at par, and the decomposition found it is also an Event of Default. The company does not have $44M.\n\n### The equity, which is the whole ballgame\n\nA company burning $2.6M a month with $11M in the bank survives by selling stock. Akoustis had three ways to do that:\n- **The at-the-market program (ATM).** The company sells shares into the market through an agent, a little each day. About $48M of capacity remained, and it re-activated the program on 13 May.\n- **An underwritten offering.** It did one in January 2024: 23M shares at $0.50, $11.5M gross, $10.4M net, priced at a 29% discount to the prior close.\n- **The shelf registration** under which both happen: $150M, about $102M unsold.\n\nThe binding constraint is not dollars; it's shares:\n- 175,000,000 authorized\n- less 98,669,282 outstanding\n- less 9,341,825 reserved for note conversion\n- less 3,031,625 for options\n- less the 5,000,000 registered for interest\n\nThat leaves **58,957,268 shares**, and every share sold comes out of that ledger. At $0.44 the ledger is worth about $26M of raising. At $0.20 it is worth about $12M. A lower share price shrinks what the company can raise from the same ledger, which is why the post-verdict price matters so much in Part 4.\n\nThe January underwriting agreement also has a lock-up, \u00a74(l): no share sales from pricing until 90 days after the closing, and the ATM isn't carved out. An offering is therefore a trade: a lump sum now, and the ATM switched off for three months.\n\n### What actually happened (the model never sees this)\n\n1. On 17 May the jury returned **$38.6M**: $31.3M unjust enrichment, $7.0M exemplary damages, $279,808 on the patents.\n2. Judgment was entered 20 May.\n3. On 24 May the company sold about $9M of stock at **$0.20**, with the lock-up.\n4. Later came fees ($11.7M) and interest ($7.3M). Trebling was denied on 15 Oct, and the post-trial motions were denied on 31 Oct.\n5. An amended judgment of $57.0M was entered 21 Nov.\n6. Chapter 11 followed on 16 Dec, and delisting on 17 Dec.\n7. The assets went to Tune Holdings, part of SpaceX, for $30.2M in 2025.\n\nThis lives in a separate outcome file. The agent, Jev and the model code are tested never to read it. It appears only in the \"what actually happened\" panel on the page.\n\n---\n\n## Part 2. The lender\n\n### Slope, reconstructed\n\nSlope does B2B payments and financing. The demo reconstructs a Slope reusable line as closely as public information allows:\n- **Limit:** 15% of trailing monthly receipts net of debt service, which is Slope's published rule. For Akoustis that's about $395k. It is above Slope's roughly $250k auto-approval, so a human reviewer would actually look at this borrower, which is the point.\n- **Price:** a 3.7% fee per draw, repaid in three monthly installments.\n- **History:** the line opened 1 Apr 2024. On 14 May it carries about $390k of principal across 8 open installments totalling about $404k.\n- **Collection:** Slope debits each installment automatically on its due date. The debit succeeds if the available balance covers it, and a failed debit is retried on the line's retry dates.\n- **Horizon:** 180 days, to 10 Nov 2024.\n\nThe bank data is synthetic, reconstructed from the company's filings (the 31 Mar balance sheet, 10-Q cash flows, the 13 May call) and labelled as synthetic once, at its source.\n\n### What a lender actually cares about\n\nThe model reports what a lender would put on a slide:\n- **Due:** installments falling due by the horizon.\n- **Collected:** what the debit actually pulled.\n- **Collection rate:** Collected \u00f7 Due.\n- **Past due:** failed debits not yet recovered.\n- **Balance at filing:** if the company files, the automatic stay (Bankruptcy Code \u00a7362) freezes whatever it still owes Slope.\n- **Preference exposure:** under \u00a7547, payments to a creditor in the 90 days before a petition can be clawed back by the estate. Every installment Slope collected in that window is at risk.\n\nThe last two are shown \"given a filing\", because a lender asks \"if it files, how bad?\" separately from \"how likely is it to file?\".\n\nNote what is *not* on the list: a recommendation. The build deliberately stops at the analysis. Whether to freeze draws or cut the limit depends on Slope's risk appetite and portfolio, which the lawsuit research doesn't establish. Putting a recommendation on the page would dress an assumption up as a finding.\n\n---\n\n## Part 3. The domain: what happens after a jury speaks\n\nThis is the machinery the model encodes. Each rule is Law (the text says so), Record (this case's documents say so), Data (measured), Arithmetic, or Scenario (a declared assumption with a sensitivity).\n\n1. **Verdict.** The jury answers the form. That's its only output.\n2. **Entry of judgment.** The court enters a money judgment, usually within days.\n3. **The automatic stay of execution, Rule 62(a).** For 30 days after entry, the winner can't collect. This is Law.\n4. **Post-trial motions, Rules 50(b) and 59.** The loser has 28 days to ask the judge to overturn the verdict (judgment as a matter of law), order a new trial, or cut the award. A cut is called **remittitur**: the court offers the plaintiff a lower amount or a new trial, and the *plaintiff* chooses. So a remittitur involves two decisions by two different parties, and the model treats it that way.\n5. **The ruling.** The judge rules on the motions: unchanged, reduced, or set aside. The time this takes is Data (this judge's measured lags on this docket), not Law.\n6. **Appeal.** A notice of appeal is due 30 days after the ruling on the last motion (FRAP 4(a)(4)).\n7. **The stay pending appeal, Rule 62(b).** Once the automatic stay ends, the loser can stop collection only by posting security, normally a bond for the whole judgment. A court can accept reduced security. In the Third Circuit that's the *Diet Drugs* line of cases, and in D. Del. *Praxis Energy Agents*. Akoustis can't bond $38M, so the question becomes what reduced security it can offer and whether the court accepts it.\n8. **Enforcement.** Without a stay, the winner can execute: levy on bank accounts and seize cash. To do that outside Delaware before the judgment is final, it needs to register the judgment elsewhere, which requires good cause under 28 U.S.C. \u00a71963.\n9. **Settlement.** It can happen at any point. The model treats it as its own offer on specified terms (12 monthly installments in the base case), accepted or not.\n10. **The notes clock (Part 1).** A final money judgment above $10M that remains unpaid and unstayed for 60 days after the 30-day automatic stay creates a default. The holders can then accelerate the notes.\n11. **Bankruptcy.** The company can file voluntarily (Chapter 11). Holders can file an involuntary petition (\u00a7303), subject to the \u00a77.06 gate.\n\nOne interpretation question is genuinely open: *which* judgment starts the \u00a77.01(i) clock? The one as entered, or the one after post-trial motions? Appeal-finality cases (*Banister*) say post-trial motions suspend finality \"for purposes of an appeal\". The indenture's test, though, is about execution, not appeal. The model's base reading is **as entered**. The **post-ruling** reading is the sensitivity. Legal meaning is never a Jev question: where research doesn't settle it, it becomes a declared scenario held consistently along each path.\n\n---\n\n## Part 4. The machine, component by component\n\n### 4.1 The evidence snapshot\n\n**Job:** decide what the system is allowed to know.\n\nA dated SQLite full-text database holds 52 sources public by 14 May 2024:\n- court filings from the free federal archive (RECAP);\n- SEC filings (the 10-Q, 10-K, the indenture, prospectuses, the 13 May resale S-3);\n- the earnings-call transcript;\n- the daily price table.\n\nAdmission is by date and content hash, and anything dated later is physically absent from the database. This is **isolation**: the agent and Jev can't cheat, because the future isn't in the room.\n\n### 4.2 The sweep\n\n**Job:** make sure nothing material in the record goes unread.\n\nBefore the agent starts, Jev screens every admissible passage (1,584 sections, split into 5,009 units) with generic questions: does this describe a legal matter, a debt obligation, a covenant, a restriction? The result is a reading list. It costs about four cents. Its purpose is that \"the agent never opened that exhibit\" stops being a way to miss the covenant that sinks the loan.\n\n### 4.3 The agent\n\n**Job:** turn documents into evidence, filed where the questions will look.\n\nThe agent is Claude (Agent SDK) with a set of tools: search, read, propose a finding, attach it to a record item, compile the dispute, compile the financing instruments, submit.\n\nIt works against **record items**, the named slots each forecast question reads. The pending-claim template has 54 of them, for example:\n- \"the parties' proposed final instructions on each claim's elements and burdens\";\n- \"the holders of record of the notes\";\n- \"counsel's statement on the ability to raise money\".\n\nThe agent's job is to fill each slot with **findings**. A finding is one statement of what one passage says, attributed to its speaker, document and status: a court ruling, a statement of intended proof, a party's argument, a company disclosure, or a third-party record.\n\nThe agent is not trusted on its own. Three checks sit on it:\n- **Every finding is checked by Jev** for support, entity and scope. A finding that says more than its passage gets rejected, and the agent narrows it.\n- **At submission, every cited paragraph is re-checked.** Every paragraph or table row the agent cited must have its payment, obligation or default terms stated by some finding.\n- **Disagreement is escalated, not overridden.** If the agent thinks the check is wrong, it escalates with a reason, and the escalation goes on a reviewer checklist, visible and non-blocking. It can't just write \"I disagree\" and move on.\n\nThe final run (`\u202620260929T052558Z`):\n- all 54 items filled;\n- 98 accepted findings, 37 with named speakers (12 Qorvo, 25 Akoustis);\n- 7 escalations, all blank verdict-form questions or the witness list;\n- 11 cents of Jev.\n\nThe agent's instructions were rewritten this week. It is told to read each item *for the event it serves*: the conditions the event depends on, and the positions and incentives of everyone who shapes it, from any author. It is not told to match document titles. Before the rewrite it missed the witness list and the noteholders. After it, it found both.\n\n### 4.4 The legal map and the question specification\n\n**Job:** decide what the model represents, before any evidence shapes it.\n\nThis follows the CORDON stage order:\n1. **Legal meaning first** (`DECOMPOSITION_20240514.md`): the law and the contracts, as a map of who decides what, when.\n2. **Clocks and parameters** from that text.\n3. **Arithmetic** from those.\n4. **Evidence fills the named slots last.**\n\nThe order matters because evidence found first tends to shape the model around itself.\n\n`QUESTIONS_20240514.md` owns the 23 questions. For each it states:\n- the **Event**: exactly what occurs;\n- the **Answers**: what each answer means, and \"no\" means only that the event does not occur;\n- the **Situation**: what is true on the path at that moment;\n- the **Record**: what evidence it reads;\n- the **Consequences**: what code books for each answer.\n\nThe governing rule: **one well-defined uncertainty, with the context that bears on it.** It is not \"one actor's decision\". \"Does the jury's trade-secret award exceed $X?\" is a fine question even though the jury isn't deciding against our threshold.\n\nThe contracts (`question_registry.json`, `dispute_model.json`) are what the code actually reads.\n\n### 4.5 Jev\n\n**Job:** the judgments nobody can compute.\n\nJev is TypeSafe's narrow judgment model (`jev-1.13`), called through OpenRouter. It returns typed answers with full probability distributions, at fractions of a cent per call, and it's cached.\n\nA question to Jev has four parts:\n- the event;\n- the answers;\n- the **situation**: the dated facts on this path, such as \"a money judgment of $X entered on <date>\", available cash, arrears, the notes' status, the share price;\n- the **evidence**: findings with author, date and status.\n\nThe shared instruction is to forecast the defined outcome at the stated time, conditional on the situation, using only the supplied evidence available by the cutoff.\n\nJev never sets an amount or a date. A reading of the evidence (\"the company intends to appeal\") is never used as a forecast probability (\"will appeal within 30 days\"). Jev's probability is labelled what it is: model judgment, not an observed frequency.\n\n### 4.6 The operating futures\n\n**Job:** the uncertainty that isn't the lawsuit.\n\nA monthly block bootstrap resamples the reconstructed bank feed into **512 trajectories** of receipts and spending over 180 days, with a fixed seed. Each trajectory is a plausible version of an ordinary six months for this company. Every scenario is run against the same 512, so differences between scenarios are the events, not noise.\n\n### 4.7 The daily cash processor\n\n**Job:** decide, every day on every trajectory, who gets paid.\n\nThis replaced a cruder \"does cash go negative?\" check this week, and it's where the lender's reality lives. Each day, in order:\n1. That day's receipts post.\n2. A levy served that day attaches first.\n3. Scheduled obligations with fixed due dates are paid in the order they were incurred: Slope's installment by automatic debit, settlement installments, the cash part of note interest. Each is paid in full or not at all.\n4. Operating spending is paid up to the balance.\n5. Whatever isn't paid becomes an **arrear** in its class (Slope, settlement, notes interest, operating).\n6. Spare balance then pays older arrears, oldest first. Slope's arrears are recovered only by its own retries.\n7. From a petition onward, everything freezes.\n\nTwo outputs matter downstream:\n- **The first unpaid day**, when the company first can't pay something.\n- **The general-nonpayment day**, from \u00a77.01(j)(v). The test is 30 continuous days of arrears in which at least a quarter of obligations that fell due went unpaid; one late bill is not \"generally not paying\". On that day the notes accelerate automatically.\n\n### 4.8 The loan engine\n\n**Job:** the loan's own cash flows.\n\nIt runs the line on each trajectory:\n- draws against the limit;\n- fees;\n- three installments per draw;\n- debits against the processor's available balance, with retries;\n- exposure outstanding;\n- the balance frozen at a filing.\n\nIt's compiled with numba, so one run across 512 trajectories takes about 4.5 ms. It has to be that fast, because the tree runs it thousands of times.\n\n### 4.9 Financing: the stock\n\n**Job:** the company's lifeline, and what the verdict does to it.\n\n- **At-the-market sales** run every trading day while the company is listed and hasn't filed.\n  - The pace is a declared scenario: 20% of the average daily dollar volume from 15 Mar to 14 May. That's about 153,213 shares a day, netting about $65,391 at $0.44 after the up-to-3% commission.\n  - Sales settle two business days later before 28 May 2024, and one business day later from that date, when the industry moved to T+1.\n  - The sensitivities are 10% and 25% of volume.\n- **The offering (N1)** is a specified deal on January's terms:\n  - $11.5M gross, January's costs, priced at the current share price less January's 29% discount;\n  - the share count is the gross over the price, and the ledger caps it;\n  - it closes (or not) on the timeline January's took;\n  - the lock-up stops the ATM from pricing until 90 days after the close.\n- **The share price after the verdict** (added today) comes from a Merton structural model.\n  - Equity is treated as a call option on the firm's value. The strike is what the company owes ahead of shareholders: the notes' $44M face, plus whatever judgment is owed.\n  - It's calibrated only on 14 May data: the $0.44 close, the share count, the stock's 113% volatility over 240 days, and the 1-year Treasury (5.16%). That gives a firm value of about $82.4M and an asset volatility of about 65%.\n  - The price moves only when the amount owed changes: at the verdict, a ruling, a settlement, a payment or a levy.\n  - Resulting prices:\n\n    | Award | Price |\n    |---|---|\n    | No award | $0.44 |\n    | $7.95M | $0.385 |\n    | $10.65M | $0.368 |\n    | $26.8M | $0.279 |\n    | $38.6M | $0.228 |\n    | $67.5M | $0.141 |\n\n  - Nothing after the cutoff went in. The actual post-verdict offering priced at $0.20, which is roughly what you'd expect at a distressed-offering discount to $0.228.\n- **The consequence.** A bad verdict now hurts three ways:\n  - the ATM brings in less per share, because shares per day stay constant and the price drops;\n  - the offering raises less;\n  - the ledger runs out sooner.\n\n  This is how the verdict chokes the financing, and it's what actually happened. The old model kept raising money at $0.44 after a $38M verdict, which no one would have bought.\n\n### 4.10 The event model and the tree\n\n**Job:** turn a sequence of answers into a sequence of dated cash events, and enumerate the sequences.\n\nThe **Chain** books events on all 512 trajectories at once: the verdict's amount, the automatic stay, motions and rulings, levies, stays and their security, settlements, offerings, the notes clock, listing outcomes, petitions. Each lands on its own day on each trajectory, because the verdict date, the ruling lag and the cash floor all vary by trajectory.\n\nThe **walker** builds the tree depth-first. At each decision it asks the question's node for its answers and branches. A **path** is one full sequence of answers. Its probability is the product of Jev's answers along it, composed by code.\n\nFive design rules keep the tree honest and finite.\n1. **Depth.** A question is asked only where its answer can change cash, timing, an obligation, or a later question inside the horizon on some trajectory. The appeal isn't asked where nothing it leads to happens before 10 Nov.\n2. **Groups.** Where trajectories on one path face different options (some can pay the judgment and some can't; some can start an offering and some can't), the question is asked separately of each group, and each trajectory takes its own group's answer. The path forks per group, carrying a mask of its trajectories.\n3. **Only impossibility deletes a branch.** A low probability never removes a feasible bad outcome.\n4. **Exact merging.** At the end, paths that book identical cash on identical trajectories under the same verdict become one path. Its edge is the exact sum of the merged paths' probabilities, so nothing is lost and the page stays small.\n5. **Five contexts stay.** Whether an appeal was filed, the notes were accelerated, Qorvo was already executing, an offering had already failed, or a stay was pending all change what a later question says. Those branches are kept even where cash is identical, because they change what Jev is judging.\n\n### 4.11 The verdict, specifically\n\nThe jury questions follow the form.\n- **Liability items first (J1):** trade secrets, conspiracy, false advertising, patents, in the form's order.\n- **Then the amounts (J1b), as thresholds.** \"Is any amount entered?\" \"Does it exceed $X?\" The cut points are where cash behaves differently:\n  - **the reach line**: the most Akoustis can pay from cash on the entry day;\n  - **the notes' $10M threshold**, which no band may straddle;\n  - **the top line**: above it, every amount books the same cash through 10 Nov, because the company files regardless.\n\nThe walk branches on the **total judgment**, which is all that later steps read (the $10M test, the bond, the levy):\n- with no trade-secret or conspiracy amount, the total is exact: $0, $279,808, $1,146,604 or $1,426,412;\n- otherwise the band books its midpoint, with its ends as the sensitivity;\n- the top band books Qorvo's claimed $67.5M.\n\n### 4.12 The rest of the questions, in plain terms\n\n- **D1:** Akoustis files post-trial motions.\n- **J2:** the court rules: unchanged, reduced, or set aside.\n- **C3:** Qorvo elects on a remittitur.\n- **D5:** Akoustis appeals.\n- **D4 / J8:** Akoustis moves for a stay; the court approves it on the security offered, which is sized on the approval day's actual balance.\n- **C1:** Qorvo enforces.\n- **J4:** the court allows early registration elsewhere.\n- **D3 / C2:** Akoustis offers the specified settlement; Qorvo accepts it.\n- **D2:** Akoustis responds to the judgment: pay, start an offering, file, or none. It's asked at entry, on each levy day, and when the default becomes available.\n- **D7:** at the cash floor (cash below a month's operating need): start an offering, file, or neither. It's asked again each time cash recovers and falls back.\n- **D8:** the first day an obligation goes unpaid: the same three options.\n- **N1:** the offering closes.\n- **D6a / D6b:** listing compliance regained by 21 Oct; failing that, a hearing requested.\n- **H1\u2013H3:** the holders accelerate; the holders petition.\n\nEach \"no\" means only that the event doesn't occur. The next action is its own question.\n\n### 4.13 The no-lawsuit view (the attribution)\n\n**Job:** answer \"how much of this is the lawsuit?\" without inventing a second lender.\n\nA lender lends once, off one forecast. So there is no bank-data-only projection sitting next to the real one; we tried that, and it compared a real forecast with an impossible one. Instead, the attribution asks the same questions on the same record, with the dispute resolved on the review date at no cost to the company. Everything else is identical: same trajectories, same burn, same financing options, same distress decisions. The difference is the lawsuit's contribution.\n\n### 4.14 Sensitivities\n\n- **Judgment sensitivity:** drag any of Jev's answers from 0% to 100% and the page reweights the saved paths instantly. No new Jev call is needed, because paths are products of answers.\n- **Economic sensitivity:** precomputed variants, each a full re-run:\n  - ATM pace;\n  - the post-ruling reading of the notes default;\n  - the nonpayment window and share;\n  - collection that protects the operating need first;\n  - a 60-day cash floor;\n  - a lump-sum settlement;\n  - cash coupon;\n  - small claims awarded nothing;\n  - the announced cost plan;\n  - an equity injection.\n- **Tornado chart:** ranks which single judgment moves Slope's collections most.\n\n### 4.15 The page\n\nOne screen:\n1. The borrower, the date, the line and the pending event.\n2. The line's dated funding, installments due, collections and remaining exposure, with distributions.\n3. How the lawsuit resolves: each verdict outcome with its probability and its cash, plus the attribution.\n4. The most consequential judgment, traced: source passage, then findings, then Jev's forecast, then the economic effect, then the loan's cash.\n5. One economic assumption beside it.\n6.
