Senior product design critique\n\n### Headline problem: the most important thing is in the smallest, last place\n\nA head of lending seeing this cold wants to know three things:\n1. How worried should I be?\n2. Why?\n3. What does it cost me?\n\nThe screen answers them in reverse order of prominence:\n- **The title is abstract.** \"100 ways the next six months could go\" names no company, no risk and no conclusion.\n- **The actual finding is buried.** The risk rises from 51 to 61 in 100 and arrives about three months earlier. That finding sits in small, regular-weight right-aligned text and in the prose paragraph below the table.\n- **The mechanism is the real insight.** An earlier trigger means the line freezes sooner, so exposure is smaller but more of it is trapped. That explanation is the last element in the section, in plain body text.\n\n**Fix:** use a declarative headline, for example: *\"Outside research raises Akoustis's bankruptcy odds to 61% by 17 Dec — and pulls the risk forward from November to August.\"* Promote the paragraph to a lede directly under it.\n\n### Clarity for a cold reader\n\n1. **Missing cast of characters.**\n   - Akoustis (the borrower) is not named until the paragraph under the table.\n   - Qorvo, \"the noteholders\", \"the verdict\" and \"the dispute\" appear in the legend with no introduction.\n   - Slope (presumably the lender, i.e. us) is never framed as \"us\".\n   - A one-line setup is needed, such as *\"Akoustis owes Qorvo $X after losing a trade-secrets verdict; its $44M notes can be called if…\"*.\n2. **\"The research\" and \"bank data\" are undefined.** What research? Whose model? Run as of when? A decision-maker needs provenance.\n3. **There is no as-of date or year.**\n   - \"Next six months\" is measured from an unstated date.\n   - \"17 Dec\" has no year.\n   - \"From August\" may already be in the past relative to when this is read.\n4. **The deltas have no valence.**\n   - −$304k invoices paid is arguably good (less exposure).\n   - −$307k collected sounds bad but is mostly a consequence of paying less.\n   - +$26k stuck in bankruptcy is bad.\n   - −$87k clawback risk is good.\n   - All of these are rendered in identical neutral text. The reader has to reason out each sign alone. Colour or label them by impact on Slope, or add a clear \"net expected loss\" row.\n5. **The table doesn't obviously reconcile.**\n   - $646k / $934k ≈ 69%, yet the next row says $87 of every $100 was collected. That is presumably because \"came due\" ≠ \"paid\".\n   - Paid − collected ($288k) doesn't map cleanly to \"stuck\" ($166k) plus anything visible.\n   - A lending head will notice this within seconds, lose trust, and start auditing rather than deciding.\n   - Either show the bridge (paid → came due → collected / stuck / outstanding) or make the definitions visible rather than hidden behind hover.\n6. **The table has no bottom line.** It lists inputs but not the number a lender actually signs off on: expected loss, or loss given default, per scenario. Put that row first and make it the biggest thing in the table.\n7. **The strip comparison is misleading at a glance.**\n   - Red \"runs short of cash\" drops from 51 to 30, which visually reads as \"the research made things safer\".\n   - In fact a new maroon cause (noteholder acceleration, 30) replaces much of it.\n   - Red and maroon are too close in hue to separate quickly.\n8. **The meaning of grey shifts.** In row 1, grey means \"keeps operating, no filing\". In row 2, the same grey means \"dispute still unresolved\", which is a materially different, still-risky state.\n9. **Jargon and hedges.**\n   - \"Futures the model weighs\" is statistician-speak.\n   - \"(one reading)\" on the 19 Aug annotation hints at legal ambiguity without explaining it. That is exactly the kind of thing a credit head will ask about, so either explain it or give the alternative date.\n\n### Hierarchy and visual design\n\n1. **Summary stats are underweighted.** \"51 in 100\" and \"61 in 100\" are the key numbers but are set in regular weight at label size. Make the numerals large and bold, and let the words be secondary.\n2. **Teal/green signals \"good\".** Using green-teal as the brand colour for \"With the research\" gives the scarier scenario a positive, safe-looking colour. It also clashes semantically with the sage-green \"Settles with Qorvo\" dots, which sit right beside the teal row label. Use a neutral accent (e.g. deep blue or ink) for the scenario identity.\n3. **The dot strips are too fine.** 100 dots in one row at about 7px, with no tick marks at 25/50/75, cannot be counted or compared precisely. Improve this in any of these ways:\n   - a 10×10 grid or 2×50 layout\n   - a tick at 50\n   - a bracket spanning the filing group with its total (\"61 file\")\n4. **Micro-categories add noise.** Salmon (1), tan (2) and blue-grey (1) are near-invisible single dots, each with its own legend entry. The legend wraps to three lines and ends with an orphan (\"Collection paused during appeal 1\") alone on the last line. Group the legend into **Files (61)** and **Doesn't file by 17 Dec (39)**, with sub-items under each, or fold the 1s and 2s into \"Other\".\n5. **Low contrast.**\n   - The light-grey dots and the \"Keeps operating\" legend swatch are barely visible on white.\n   - The grey subtitle and table header text are also light.\n   - Likely WCAG failures for small text.\n6. **Tooltip affordance is inconsistent and hidden.**\n   - Four of the five row labels have dotted underlines; \"Collected by 17 Dec\" does not.\n   - Hover-only definitions also vanish in screenshots, PDFs and board packs, which is where this will likely end up.\n7. **Mixed units in the table columns.** $k values and \"$X of every $100\" share a column. It works, but the ratio row would read faster as a percentage, or visually separated as a rate.\n8. **Minor right-edge misalignment.** The table's last-column values and header end about 10px short of the rule's right edge and of the right-aligned summaries above (\"…by 17 Dec\" ends flush at about x=1168). Pick one right edge.\n9. **Wide empty gutter.** Content stops at about 1,168px in a wider window. That is acceptable for an editorial measure, but the table could reclaim some width, or the page could be centred.\n\n### Bugs and visual slop\n\n1. **Annotation collision in Section 2.** The label \"19 Aug · Noteholders' 60-day clock ends (one reading)\" runs from about x=405 to x=716. The dashed leader for \"21 Oct\" drops at about x=698, straight through \"reading)\". This is a clear overlap. Either shorten the label (move \"(one reading)\" to a footnote), wrap it, or add a third stagger level.\n2. **Tight stagger.** The 8 Aug and 19 Aug labels are only 11 days apart and already need two lines. On a narrower viewport these will collide further, so the annotation layout needs responsive handling or collision detection.\n3. **Leader offset.** The dashed leaders sit about 5px left of their label text starts. That may be intentional, but it reads as slightly misregistered.\n4. **The Section 2 cut-off is only the viewport**, not a layout bug. Still, it is worth checking that the chart renders beneath the annotations with adequate spacing, because the labels are currently floating with nothing visible to attach to.\n\n### Prioritised recommendations\n\n1. Rewrite the headline as the conclusion, and move the mechanism paragraph up as the lede.\n2. Add a two-sentence context block: who Akoustis, Qorvo and the noteholders are, what \"the research\" is, and the as-of date.\n3. Add an **expected loss / net impact** row at the top of the table, and colour deltas by good or bad for Slope.\n4. Show or bridge the table definitions inline so the numbers visibly reconcile.\n5. Group the second legend into Files vs. Doesn't file, and differentiate red from maroon more strongly.\n6. Replace the teal \"research\" accent with a neutral colour, and fix the grey-means-two-things issue.\n7. Fix the 19 Aug / 21 Oct label overlap, and add responsive collision handling for the annotations.\n\n**Overall:** the craft is good. It has a restrained palette, a consistent scenario colour, clean table rules and a strong typographic voice. But it is built like a reader-paced explainer, not a decision document. For a head of lending, the answer, the dollar impact and the \"why\" need to come first. The table also has to reconcile at a glance, or the conversation will be about the numbers instead of the decision."
